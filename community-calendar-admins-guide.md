# Community calendars: a guide for admins

Berkeley Moshav's community-wide events, such as community meetings, are
scheduled on community calendars. Each community calendar has a small group
of admins, who schedule its events. This guide is for them.

(Members' own social events go on the shared community calendar instead, by
inviting share@berkeleymoshav.org.)

## What each calendar has

- **The calendar itself**, named for its events (for example "Community
  Meetings"). Its admins can add and change events on it. Everyone else sees
  its events on the **All Berkeley Moshav Events** calendar, which shows every
  group's meetings and every community-wide event.
- **An email list for its admins**, named after the calendar, ending in
  "Admins" (for example community-meetings-admins@berkeleymoshav.org). The
  links to the calendar, its Zoom room, its Drive folder, the admin group
  in Gather, and the Zoom host key are in the footer at the bottom of every
  message to the list.
- **A Zoom room** (for example "Community Meetings Zoom Room"): the
  calendar's permanent meeting place.
- **A Drive folder**, for the admins' documents, in the Meeting Admin folder
  (for example "Community Meetings Admins").
- **An admin group in Gather.** Its managers can add and remove admins, and
  make other admins managers. Admins are added to the email list and the
  Drive folder within the hour.

## Adding the calendar to your own Google Calendar

Click the "Google calendar" link in the footer of any message to the admins'
list, then click **Add**.

## Scheduling an event

Create the event on the community calendar itself, not on your own calendar:

1. In Google Calendar, create the event as usual, with its title, time, and
   description.
2. In the event's details, choose the community calendar in the list of
   calendars. That list is next to the calendar icon, and most likely shows
   your own name.
3. Don't add Zoom details or guests: they're added for you.
4. Click **Save**.

If you invite the community calendar from an event on your own calendar
instead, the community calendar only gets a copy of your invitation. A copy
can't be given the Zoom room, and the community isn't invited. You'll get an
email asking you to create the event on the community calendar instead.

## What's added automatically

A few minutes after you save an event, it gets:

- **The calendar's Zoom room**: its link as the event's location, and the
  link, meeting ID and passcode in its description.
- **The whole community invited** (all.members@berkeleymoshav.org): each member
  gets the invitation by email, and the event appears on their calendar.
- **A place on the All Berkeley Moshav Events calendar.**

When you later change an event's time, Google Calendar asks whether to email
the guests about it. The guests are the whole community, so say yes for a
change people need to know about.

## Reminders

Everyone invited is emailed a reminder 24 hours before each event, with the
event's time, Zoom link, and passcode. Replies to a reminder come to the
admins' list. All-day events get no reminder.

## Changing what an event gets

Type one of these tags in the event's title or description:

| Tag | What it does |
|---|---|
| `[no zoom]` | No Zoom room: for an event held in person, or elsewhere. An event with another Zoom or Google Meet link typed in keeps that link, and doesn't get the room either. |
| `[no invite]` | The community isn't invited, and gets no reminder: for an event only some people should come to. Add it before you first save the event: once the community has been invited, remove all.members@berkeleymoshav.org from the guests by hand. |
| `[remind 2 hours]` | A reminder at another time: any length of time, such as `[remind 90 minutes]` or `[remind 2 days]`, or several, such as `[remind 1 day, 1 hour]`. With `[no invite]`, the reminder still goes to the community. |
| `[no reminder]` | No reminder. |

## The Zoom room

The room's link never changes, and anyone with the link can join, with or
without a host. The room isn't recorded unless a host records it.

For host features, such as breakout rooms, muting, or recording, the event's
host claims host with the room's host key: in Zoom, click Participants, then
Claim Host, and enter the key. If someone else becomes host by accident, ask
them to make you host ("Make Host").

The host key is in the "Community Events Zoom Host Key" doc, linked from the
"Zoom host key" line in the footer of every message to the admins' list. Only
admins can open it. Please keep it that way:

- Don't share the key, or forward the doc, to anyone who isn't an admin. With
  the key, anyone can take over any community event's meeting.
- Don't put the key, or the doc's link, in an event's description: events are
  sent to the whole community.
- If someone asks for access to the doc, don't grant it from the doc. If they
  should be an admin, a manager of the admin group adds them to it in Gather,
  which gives them access within the hour.

All the community calendars' rooms share one host key. If it leaks, tell the
Technology Circle, who will change it.

## When events clash

All the community calendars' rooms belong to one Zoom user, which can host only
one meeting at a time. So if an event overlaps another community-wide event,
or is less than 15 minutes from one (in case the first runs over), the admins
of both calendars get an email, asking them to agree which one moves. To find
a free time, check the All Berkeley Moshav Events calendar.

Community-wide events don't clash with circles' and other groups' meetings,
which use other Zoom users.
